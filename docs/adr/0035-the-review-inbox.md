# ADR-0035: The review inbox: a list with roving focus, focus-scoped single-key shortcuts, one message per action, the batch checkpoint, likely-edit tiers, first-person uncertainty, and diff cards

## Status

Accepted (2026-11-25; proposed 2026-11-20). Phase 5 WP4 drafted the likely-edit tiers and first-person uncertainty (below): `tierOf`, the tiers' $0 budgets in `checks`, the copy, and `tierOf` in place of WP1's stand-in in every writer. WP5 built the inbox's structure (below): `/inbox`, its loader, the list of cards with roving focus, the focus-scoped single-key shortcuts and Settings → Review's switch, one message per action, the focus rules, the review clock, and Reviewed, Snoozed, and Finishing. WP6 added "accept all" with its checkpoint, the tray on the inbox's card, and the inbox's count in the header and on Today (below). WP12 added diff cards (below): each update's value when the rep spoke beside its value now, from the mirror's history (ADR-0008), the 3-way card with Keep mine, Keep the CRM's, and Edit, the re-link offer on new records at review time, both loud though neither is a flag, and the demo controls that make them happen on demo records. WP13 gave the correction panel a field picker and a text box, and added follow-up cards (ADR-0038). Two things are the maintainer's: the loud tier is 15.8% (119 of 751) against the spec's 15%, gated at an interim 18% ceiling while asks, conflicts, and unsupported values alone are 15.7%, so keep the ceiling or ask for fewer false witness conflicts first; and G3b, the manual screen-reader passes (spec WP16), which also answer the questions only a screen reader can (spec Appendix B, #3 and #4). Amends ADR-0031 (its "Phase 5: the likely-edit tiers").

## Context and problem

Phase 4 writes flags on proposals: fourteen kinds, from the linker, the normalizers, the faithfulness check, and the second witness. Phase 4's tray showed every flag as a badge, and on the 200 debriefs 45.8% of proposals carried at least one (ADR-0031), against a 35% guard. A sea of badges teaches reviewers to ignore them:

- Highlights helped only when precise. Edit-likelihood highlights in Vasconcelos et al. (TOCHI 2024) marked tokens of which 64.7% were edited, and cut review time; generation-probability highlights tuned to the same density had 26.1% edited, and gave no benefit. Raw speech-recognition confidence didn't help either (Kuhn et al., CHI EA 2025). QE4PE (TACL 2025) found the highlighted share drove editing time.
- Wrong accounts are recall-critical: a reviewer who learns that unmarked means safe will miss the one that isn't (Xu, Lien, and Höllerer, CHI 2023, on high-recall suggestions).
- Phrasing doubt in the first person cut agreement with a wrong AI and raised accuracy (Kim et al., FAccT 2024), but there it sat on a random half of the answers; over-flagging buys underreliance, since accuracy on right answers fell.

So the cards need marks that are rare where they're loud, still present where an error would otherwise hide, and computed the same way by every part of Earshot that decides what a reviewer sees or what "accept all" takes.

The inbox around them has to be fast from the keyboard (a 4 s median per proposal is the gate, and a keystroke saves about a second against the mouse), and usable with a screen reader and by speech input. Single-key shortcuts are what make review fast, and what WCAG 2.2 SC 2.1.4 constrains: they must be possible to turn off, or act only on focus. Screen readers in browse mode take letters and digits for themselves (NVDA: k, a, e, r, u, 1–6). The capture screen already binds Space to push-to-talk while a debrief is docked. Reviews are written by the web route after the seal (ADR-0032), so the page must survive a refusal, and the pilot needs each review's time (spec §4.8).

## Considered options

- **What decides a mark:** **two tiers from Phase 4's flags and decisions, by one rule table (chosen)**; one confidence threshold (raw confidence didn't help); a learned likely-edit model (Phase 8, from approve, edit, and reject signals); token-level marks (they raised agreement with the AI in Villavicencio et al., FAccT 2026).
- **`link_uncertain`** (ADR-0031's options): **option 2, a note beside the record's name, now (chosen)**; option 1, moving its bar for context links to p below 0.90, after Phase 4's mined set measures its real precision; leaving it a badge.
- **The loud budget, when display rules can't meet it:** **gate an interim ceiling at the measured floor plus 2 points, and fix the false conflicts later (chosen; the orchestrator's decision under spec §9)**; changing the witness threshold now; leaving loud ungated until the floor falls.
- **The first-person line:** **templates on loud cards only (chosen)**; hedges written by the model; the general phrasing ("It's not clear…"), whose effects were weaker and not significant.
- **The inbox's structure (WP5):** **an `<ol>` of `<article>` cards with native buttons and roving focus (chosen)**; `feed` (for reading in browse mode, with Page Up and Down); `listbox` (an option can't hold buttons); `grid` (verbose, and two dimensions the inbox doesn't need yet).
- **Single-key shortcuts:** **on by default, active only while focus is in the list and not in a text field, with a per-device switch (chosen)**; off by default, as Gmail's are (it hides the fastest path from everyone); global, as Gmail's are once on.
- **Announcing actions:** **one polite toast per action through Base UI's toaster, urgent for a failure, and no other live region (chosen)**; a status region of the inbox's own beside the toaster (two announcements per action).
- **Undo's home:** **the toast, u, and a Reviewed section that keeps Undo until the sync window closes, then Reopen (chosen)**; the toast alone (gone in 5 s, so it fails WCAG 2.2.1 for anyone slower).

## Decision

### The tiers

`tierOf(proposal)` in `@earshot/understanding/tiers` returns `{ tier, reasons, nameNote }` (spec §4.4). It reads the proposal as the projection folds it (witness downgrades and later flags included) or as it was written: its flags, its target's decision, basis, and candidates, and each field's `norm`.

| Tier | When | Shown as | Counts toward |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Loud** | The target is an ask, the linker's (`ambiguous_target`) or a witness downgrade; any `witness_conflict`; `unsupported` on the target or a field; `ambiguous_value`; a `value_mismatch` nothing fixed (no `norm.llmValue`: a self-correction whose retracted value the model kept, a name or email spelled otherwise, an amount in another currency); a `new_entity` with a sound-alike, a record candidate at p ≥ 0.05 (the tray's `ASK_MIN_P`) | Amber, a first-person line, choices on 1/2/3, kept out of "accept all" and policies | Loud share, marked share, surfacing |
| **Quiet, on the card** | `implied_target`; a `value_mismatch` a unique reading fixed ("corrected from …"); `negation`; `low_confidence`; `weak_support`; a `new_entity` with no sound-alike; v1's `date` and `number` | A short note on the field or the card, no color | Marked share, surfacing |
| **Quiet, by the name** | `link_uncertain` on a linked target | "matched by sound" or "matched in context", beside the record's name | Name-note share, surfacing; not the marked share |
| **None** | No flag, or only `self_correction` (the model took the correction) | Nothing | — |

- **Reasons** come loud first, each tier in a fixed order (a conflict, then the linker's question, unsupported words, a sound-alike, two readings, an unfixed mismatch; then the quiet ones), then in the flags' order. The first loud reason picks the card's line. A downgrade is a conflict, not a second question; a target that's an ask with no flag saying so is loud anyway.
- **The name note** belongs beside a record's name, so only a target still linked carries it; a downgraded one is a question, and loud already. It makes the tier quiet, so "accept all" and policies leave the card, but the evals count it apart.
- **Every kind has a role.** `tierOf` restates `RiskKindV2` (the package can't import `@earshot/core` without Web Crypto types), and a type test in `@earshot/evals` fails when the two differ, as does an exhaustive switch: a new kind can't arrive without a tier.
- **One function everywhere.** `SessionDO` (`TIER_OF`: what its own reviews record as seen, and the rules for the socket's reviews), the web Worker's review route (`writeSessionReviews`' `tierOf`, which also defaults to it), the in-memory `SessionDO` in `@earshot/voice/testing`, the tray, the evals, and, as WP5, WP7, and WP11 built them, the inbox, the pilot builder, and policies. WP1's stand-in, `anyFlagTier` (any flag loud), survives only as a test helper in `@earshot/events/testing`, for the writer rules' own tests. With the real tiers a lone `self_correction` is unmarked, so "accept all" takes it.
- **Pure.** It imports types alone from the linker and the faithfulness check, so its bundle is its own file (about 3.4 KB unminified, 1.8 KB minified), for the edge Worker, the web Worker, and the browser.
- A record offered at review time (WP12) also makes a card loud, so "accept all" leaves it; it isn't a flag, and the evals don't count it.

### The budgets, measured before anything changed

On the 200 debriefs' 751 final proposals, both adapters alike (spec WP4 step 2):

| Share | Value |
|---|---|
| Loud | 15.8% (119) |
| The loud floor: asks, conflicts, and unsupported values alone | **15.7% (118)**: 68 witness conflicts (9.1%), 32 linker asks (4.3%), 20 unsupported targets (2.7%), with 2 proposals both an ask and a conflict |
| Loud from the rest | 1 proposal: a new record with a sound-alike at p between 0.05 and 0.20 |
| Marked (loud or a card note) | 30.1% (226) |
| The name note alone | 15.7% (118) |
| Quiet card notes, by reason | 40 new records with no sound-alike, 32 negations, 24 implied targets, 20 values the normalizers fixed |
| Surfacing (seeded errors marked) | 100% (40 of 40) |

**The floor is over 15%,** so no display rule can bring loud under the spec's budget. Spec §9 would stop and ask; the orchestrator decided instead (2026-11-20):

- **The loud gate is an interim ceiling, 18%:** the floor plus 2 points, rounded up to a whole percent (`LOUD_SHARE_INTERIM_CEILING` in `packages/evals/src/understanding/tiers.ts`). A test fails when the floor moves, so the ceiling is set again, or dropped for 15%, on purpose.
- **The real fix is fewer false witness conflicts:** 22 of the 68 conflicts aren't ones the scripts expect, and 19 of those turned right links into asks (ADR-0031). That's a linker or witness change, after Phase 4's mined set runs (its WP6). WP4 changed no linker or witness threshold.
- **The loud `new_entity` bar stays at p ≥ 0.05.** The spec raises it to 0.20 only when the floor is under 15% and loud isn't; here it would quiet one proposal and change nothing about the budget.
- The 200 debriefs over-represent hard cases by design (48 snapped names, 20 paraphrased targets), so their loud share is an upper reference for real use.

**The gates** (`eval:understanding --check`, in `checks`): loud ≤ 18% (interim; target 15%), marked ≤ 35%, surfacing ≥ 95%, for each provider. **Reported:** the floor, the name-note share, per-tier precision, and wrong accounts with no marker.

**Precision** (the share the ground truth says needs a choice, an edit, or a rejection; a question offering the record the ground truth names outright is a false alarm):

| Tier | Precision |
|---|---|
| Loud | 65.5% (78 of 119), over the 50% aim. By reason: unsupported 100% (20/20), conflicts 69.1% (47/68), asks 37.5% (12/32: 20 are linker v1 asking where the scripts' context rule links), the sound-alike 0% (0/1) |
| Quiet, card notes | 0% (0 of 107): by construction, since the cassettes' quiet notes sit on right values |
| The name note alone | 0.8% (1 of 118): the d193 wrong account, marked only by its note |

**Hidden wrong accounts: 1**, d037's snapped new name, linked by context at p 0.998, above `link_uncertain`'s 0.97, so nothing marks it (ADR-0028's known gap, near-miss names that link anyway).

### First-person uncertainty

`apps/web/lib/inbox/voice.ts` holds one template per loud reason, in the UI's words, never written by a model, and never on a quiet or unmarked card (spec decision 9). The line's first sentence is also the card's accessible description: "Task, Acme Corp, needs a check: I'm not sure I caught the company."

| Reason | Line |
|---|---|
| Witness conflict on the target | "I'm not sure I caught the company. Acme or Akme?" (the name, the deal, the record, by the target's kind) |
| Witness conflict on a field | "I'm not sure I caught the name. “Dana Lee” or “Dana Li”?" |
| The linker asked | "I'm not sure which company you meant. Acme Dynamics or Akme Dynamics?" ("who you meant" for a person) |
| Unsupported target | "I couldn't find the company's name in what you said, so I haven't picked a record." |
| Unsupported field | "I couldn't find the words for the due date in what you said." |
| Ambiguous value | "I heard “next Friday”. Did you mean the 27th or the 4th?" |
| Mismatch not fixed, after a self-correction | "You corrected yourself here, and I may have kept the first date: March 6." |
| Mismatch not fixed, otherwise | "I may not have written the email the way you said it: dana@acme.io." |
| New, with a sound-alike | "I didn't find “Atmo Systems” in your CRM. Did you mean Atmos Industrial, or is it new?" |
| Re-link offer (WP12) | "Acme Robotics was added to your CRM after you spoke. Is it this one?" |

Two lines depart from the spec's examples, for the data's sake: an unsupported target names its kind, not the model's words, because the words the transcript doesn't hold aren't kept on the proposal; and an unfixed mismatch with no self-correction (a name, an email, a stage, or another currency) gets its own line, since the spec's covers only the correction.

The quiet notes sit beside `RISK_LABELS` (`apps/web/lib/capture/copy.ts`): "Open account assumed", "Corrected from March 6" (the value a reading replaced), "Said in a negation: “…”" (the words), "Creates a record", and the rest; the name notes are "matched by sound" and "matched in context". The capture tray already shows the name note in the card's heading and no longer shows `link_uncertain` as a badge; WP5 and WP6 put the rest on the inbox's card.

### The inbox (WP5)

**Where it is.** `/inbox` (`apps/web/app/(app)/inbox/page.tsx`) reads the inbox on the server and hands it to one client view, `InboxView` (`components/inbox/inbox-view.tsx`). `GET /api/inbox` (same-site, `no-store`) reads it again, for polling and "Load older". Both run `readInbox` (`lib/inbox/read.ts`): the repair path first (`repairUnsealedSessions`, ADR-0032, with `sealSessions` given at most 3 s so an unreachable edge Worker can't hold the page), then the loader.

**The loader** (`lib/inbox/load.ts`, in the viewer's `withTenant` transaction): the viewer's own latest 20 debriefs that have proposals (target), newest first, with a cursor for "Load older" (start time and id). For each: its `proposals` rows, which `rebuildProjections` writes one `ProposalView` each, read back as views with only the parts of the latest review the page needs (id, decision, record, mode, batch, what it undoes, `syncEligibleAt`, time); the utterances their quotes come from; and every record the cards name (targets, candidates, the company said with a target, the focus account), each with its account (a contact's or deal's company, found as the session routes find parents) and the values an update compares against (a deal's stage, amount, and close date; a contact's title, email, and phone). One query per kind of thing, never one per session.

**The sections and groups** (`lib/inbox/groups.ts`, pure, rerun on every render so the page's own reviews move cards at once):

| Section | What's in it |
|---|---|
| Needs review | A sealed debrief's proposals still `proposed` and not snoozed: one group per debrief, headed "Acme Corp · today, 2:14 pm" by its focus account, else the account its proposals name most, else "Debrief"; a sub-heading per account when its cards name more than one (a question's account is the one company all its records belong to, else none) |
| Finishing | A debrief that isn't sealed yet: "Finishing, usually under a minute" once ended, "Still being recorded" before; polled every 10 s (target) while the page is visible, each poll running the repair path |
| Reviewed | Every decided proposal, newest first, with Undo until its `syncEligibleAt` (a rejection: 30 s after it), then Reopen; both reopen the proposal |
| Snoozed | Proposals snoozed into the future, soonest first, with "Review now" (a snooze with no time) |

The time is the reviewer's own: the server renders it in UTC, saying so, and the page swaps in "today, 2:14 pm" as it hydrates (`useSyncExternalStore`), so the two never disagree mid-hydration.

**The list.** Each group's cards are an `<ol>` labelled by its heading (with the account's sub-heading when there is one), so a screen reader announces "list, Acme Corp · today, 2:14 pm, 4 items". Each card is a `<li><article tabindex aria-labelledby aria-describedby>` (`components/inbox/proposal-card.tsx`): the heading names the type and the record ("Task · Acme Corp", with the name note beside a near link); a loud card adds the amber marker ("Needs a check") and its first-person line, which is also its description ("Task, Acme Corp, needs a check: I'm not sure I caught the company."); quiet notes are short muted lines, on their field or the card; unmarked cards are plain. Fields are diff rows: the CRM's value, an arrow, the proposed value for an update ("Appointment scheduled → proposal"), and "Heard: …" under every field. The actions are a Base UI Toolbar of native buttons (Accept, Edit, Reject, and More: three snoozes and Correct), each with `aria-keyshortcuts` while the shortcuts are on.

**Choices, and why a digit never changes a record.** A card's numbered choices (`lib/inbox/card.ts`, a Base UI Radio Group, 1–3, and 4 when the linker offered three records and "New") appear only where its first loud reason has something to choose: a question's records with their odds, a new record's sound-alikes and "New", a field's two readings when the witnesses disagree, or an ambiguous value's readings. Picking one with the pointer or the arrow keys only selects it; a digit, or Accept, decides. A linked card shows no records at all: its other candidates appear only in the correction panel (c, or More → Correct), where a digit chooses one and approves with it (`target` set). Accept on a question with nothing picked says "Choose a record first" and moves focus to the choices; a card with no record can't be accepted and says why.

**Roving focus** (`use-roving-focus.ts`): one card is in the tab order (`tabIndex` 0), the rest -1. j and k move across groups and don't wrap; Tab goes into the active card's toolbar (one stop, arrows inside); focus arriving in a card any other way makes it the active one. Every move lands on a card or the "All caught up" heading, never `<body>`.

**Focus after a card leaves** (`lib/inbox/focus.ts`, pure): the next card in its group; the previous, if it was the last; the next group's first card; then, departing from the spec's list, the last card of the group before when only earlier groups have cards left (going straight to "All caught up" would say nothing's left while something is); then the "All caught up" heading, which takes focus. Undo brings the card back and focuses it. Focus moves in the same render the card leaves in, before paint (within the 100 ms target). A card's menu would return focus to its trigger as it closes; the page tells it not to when the action moved focus on (`finalFocus`).

**The shortcuts** (`lib/inbox/keys.ts`, `use-inbox-keys.ts`): j and k move; a accepts; e edits; r rejects; s snoozes until tomorrow at 9:00 local time (More offers three hours or next Monday at 9:00); c opens the correction panel (on a question, its choices); u undoes the latest thing done here that still stands; 1–4 choose and accept; ? opens the help, which carries the NVDA+Space advice. No Space, no ⌘↵, no modifiers. One handler sits on the element holding the lists and the "All caught up" heading, so a key acts only while focus is inside it. It ignores keys from text fields and anything editable, from inside an open editor (which keeps Enter and Esc), from portals React bubbles up (a card's menu), and, inside the correction panel, everything but its digits. Settings → Review (`app/(app)/settings/review/page.tsx`) turns them off per device (`earshot.inbox.shortcuts` in localStorage, read through `useSyncExternalStore`); off, no single key acts, the keys leave the buttons, and so does `aria-keyshortcuts`.

**Editing** (`edit-form.tsx`, `lib/inbox/edit.ts`): the fields become labelled inputs (dates and times the browser's own controls, an ambiguous value's readings as radio choices, long text a textarea). Each error is tied to its field (`aria-invalid`, `aria-describedby`): a number that isn't one, a day that doesn't exist, a time that isn't `HH:MM`, or an edit that changes nothing. Enter saves as `edited` with the changed fields as `editDiff`; Esc cancels; focus returns to the card either way.

**Messages.** Each action says one polite thing through Base UI's toaster (priority `low`), replacing the last: "Accepted: task for Acme Corp. 4 left. Press U to undo." (the key only while the shortcuts are on), with an Undo button. A refusal says one urgent thing (priority `high`). The inbox has no live region of its own: the e2e checks `main` holds none. A focused card keeps clear of the toast and the header as it scrolls in (`scroll-margin`).

**Sinks, and optimism** (`lib/inbox/sinks.ts`): every action goes through a `ReviewSink`, here `routeSink`, `POST /api/sessions/:id/reviews`; WP6's tray passes the socket, WP7's pilot a local sink. `OptimisticReviews` applies each change to the page at once, sends the changes one at a time in order (so an undo never overtakes the review it reverses), and on a refusal brings the card back as it was, drops anything queued after it for the same proposal, and reports it once. The page asks before the tab closes while anything is still on its way. A fresh load reconciles what it already holds.

**What each review records** (`lib/inbox/review.ts`): a UUIDv7 `reviewId` from the page; `mode: "single"`; the record chosen, if any; `seen` (the card's tier, each field's proposed value with the CRM's value as `base` for an update, which WP12 now tells from `current` (below), the record shown and the records offered, and `clip: "none"` until WP9); `timing` from the review clock; `input` from the event that made it (a key, a click with no pointer type for Enter or Space on a button, a pointer, or touch); `surface: "inbox"`. Undo and Reopen send a reopen naming the latest review, `reason: "undo"`, with zero timing, since no card is on screen to time. The route adds `by` and `syncEligibleAt`.

**The review clock** (`lib/inbox/clock.ts`, pure on an injected clock; `use-review-clock.ts` feeds it). It runs for the active card (the first on load) while it's at least half visible (an IntersectionObserver), the page is visible, and the reviewer has given a key, pointer, wheel, scroll, or touch input in the last 30 s (target); the 30 s before a pause count, the gap after doesn't. Each card keeps its own time across moves. `decisionMs` is its time at the first decision key or click (a, e, r, s, c, a digit, or their buttons); `handlingMs` at the commit: the same moment for a, r, s, and digits, the save for e, the choice for c. A commit hands both over and starts the card at zero. It arms nothing until the page mounts, so it renders on the server.

**Development's "Finishing".** Zero-setup development runs no flush, so nothing ever finishes there. Under `next dev` only, Settings → Data offers "Load a finishing debrief", which imports a third sample unsealed (`importSessionLog`'s `seal: false`, ending it now), so the state can be seen and tested; the repair path seals it after 10 minutes.

### "Accept all", the tray, and navigation (WP6)

**One engine, two lists.** WP5's page logic moved into a hook, `useReviewList` (`components/inbox/use-review-list.ts`): the optimistic changes, the sections and card models, roving focus, the review clock, the keys, the messages, the focus rules, undo, and "accept all". `InboxView` keeps what's the inbox's own (the loader's data, polling a finishing debrief, "Load older") and renders; the capture screen's tray renders the same engine over its live debrief. A toast's Undo reads its action when it's pressed (`toastActions`, a ref set after every render), never from the render that announced it, which still saw the card as it was (the fix WP5's PR got after review, applied here to the batch too).

**What "accept all" takes** (`lib/inbox/batch.ts`, pure): a debrief's open cards (proposed, not snoozed) with nothing of the page's still on its way (`OptimisticReviews.pendingIds`), that `tierOf` leaves unmarked (`none`), and that can be accepted as they stand: with a record, since `checkReview` refuses to approve a card with none (`no_target`) and a question is always loud. That's the writers' rule exactly: a test runs every card of a fixture with each tier through `checkReview` in batch mode, with the same `tierOf`, and the page's set equals the set the rule accepts (the one difference, a card with a change on its way, is stricter on the page's side). Everything else in the debrief stays, each with why: loud ("Needs a check: I'm not sure who you meant."), a quiet note ("Has a note: Open account assumed"), the name note ("Record matched in context"), no record, or still saving.

**The button and the checkpoint** (`components/inbox/accept-all.tsx`). In each debrief's header, "Accept 3 unmarked" (`aria-expanded`, `aria-controls`). With nothing unmarked it stays in the tab order (Base UI's `focusableWhenDisabled`, so `aria-disabled`), and says why beside it and as its description: "Every card here is marked, so each needs a look." It opens the checkpoint inline, under the header, structural rather than a dialog (spec decision 3): a `fieldset` named "Accept all from this call", its summary ("3 will be accepted. 1 stays for review."), the two lists with what each card is and why each stays, "Accept 3" and Cancel. Focus moves to "Accept 3", which the summary describes, so a screen reader hears what will be accepted and what stays as the checkpoint opens (WP16's ninth check). Esc or Cancel closes it and returns focus to the header's button; the list's single keys ignore everything inside it (`data-inbox-checkpoint`).

**What a batch writes.** One `proposal.reviewed` v2 per card, approved as proposed (`target: null`), `mode: "batch"`, one `batchId` (a UUIDv7), `seen` as a single review's, `input` from the confirming event, `surface` where it happened, and every item's `timing` the batch's own (spec §4.8): `decisionMs` and `handlingMs` both the active time from opening the checkpoint to confirming it, on its own `ReviewClock` (`batchClock`), fed by the page's visibility and input as a card's is, so a hidden page or 30 s of idling pauses it. The cards' clocks pause while the checkpoint is open, and each card the batch takes starts again from zero if an undo brings it back.

**Requests, and a batch refused partway** (`lib/inbox/sinks.ts`). A change is now one action over one or more cards (`Change.items`), applied in one update, sent as one queued change, and reported as one failure. A change bigger than one request (25 items, or 64 KiB, sized as `splitReviews` sizes a socket frame, which a route body of the same reviews is smaller than) is split, in order, each part a separate all-or-nothing write; a batch keeps its `batchId` across them (`checkReviews` allows a batch over several requests with one id). The first refusal stops the rest. **The parts that landed stay**: the log holds their approvals, so showing those cards as open would be false, and they sync after `syncEligibleAt` unless undone; the refused part and anything unsent come back as they were, with one urgent message ("Accepted 25 of 30. Couldn't accept the other 5: one of them changed since the page loaded. Reload to see them as they are now."). A change queued behind it that built on a card that came back (the batch's undo, say) loses that card's part and goes out for the rest; one left with nothing is dropped. The batch's undo then reverses exactly the part that landed. Rejected: compensating the landed part with reopens, so the batch would be all or nothing from the page's side (more writes, more ways to fail, and against what the reviewer asked for); and sending one oversized request (the route and the socket both refuse it).

**Undo.** One u, the toast's Undo, or Reviewed's one row for the batch reverses it: a reopen per card the batch still stands on (`batchItems`: its latest review is the batch's approval), each naming that card's own review, as `checkReview` requires, in `batch` mode with the batch's own id ("a batch approves or undoes"), zero time, sent as one change. A card reopened or decided on its own since isn't the batch's any more: the undo leaves it alone, and Reviewed shows it on its own row. Reviewed groups a batch's cards into one row, "Accepted together: 3 proposals: …", with one Undo (then Reopen, after `syncEligibleAt`), read from the log after a reload as well as from the page.

**Focus** (`lib/inbox/focus.ts`). Several cards leaving at once follow §4.8's rules for the group as one removal (`focusAfterBatch`): the batch is confirmed from the header, above the cards, so the group is read again from its start: the first card the batch left in its group (what stays, top first), else the next group's first card, else an earlier group's last, else "All caught up". After the undo, focus lands on the batch's first card, as the list shows them once they're back. A refusal that brings cards back after the list emptied moves focus from the departing "All caught up" heading to the first of them (`useRovingFocus.rescue`), so a failure never drops focus on `<body>` either.

**Messages.** One polite message for the batch, replacing the last, with Undo: "Accepted 3. 1 stays for review. Press U to undo." (or "4 left." when nothing in the debrief stays); "Back for review: 3 proposals. 7 left." for its undo; one urgent message for a failure, as above.

**The tray** (`components/capture/proposals-tray.tsx`). On the live capture screen the tray renders the inbox's card (heading level 3) through the same engine, with `surface: "tray"` and a socket sink (`socketSink`): a request's reviews as one `review` message through `SessionClient.sendReview`, each snooze through `sendSnooze`. It shows the debrief's open cards (reviewable whether or not it's sealed), the tray's Reviewed and Snoozed, "All caught up", and the inbox's help on ?. Cards still slide in with their View Transition, mark their first paint for the turn's latency, and mark their quoted words in the transcript on hover and focus. A replay (`/demo`) keeps the read-only card that shows review status. "Accept all" stays in the inbox's header (the spec puts it there; the tray could take it later at no cost, since the engine has it).

**Across `end`, the seal, and the hand-over** (`lib/capture/controller.ts`). The session client is wired with the review route (`createReviewRoute`), and it's the tray's writer (`CaptureUiState.reviews`). After `end` the controller keeps it open (it used to close it a few seconds after `end`'s late events) until `session.sealed` arrives over the socket, until the rep leaves the capture screen (`leaveScreen`, on the screen's last unmount), or until 31 minutes have passed (the seal's 30-minute cap and a margin); then it closes once what's in flight is delivered. Meanwhile the tray reviews over the socket, and each review restarts `SessionDO`'s 60 s grace. On `sealed` the screen's state says so (`sealed`), the tray says "This debrief is done. Review what's left in the inbox." with a link, and its reviews go to the web route: one the socket refuses with `sealed`, and every one once the client has closed, under the same `reviewId`, so whichever writer takes it writes it once. Right after the seal, Postgres may not hold it yet: the route answers 409 `not_sealed`, the client retries briefly, and a card it still can't record comes back with "its debrief is still finishing". Zero-setup development has no flush, so there the hand-over ends at `not_sealed`.

**The capture screen's shortcuts** (`components/capture/use-capture-shortcuts.ts`). H, P, Esc, and ? ignore keys from the review list (`data-review-list`), from a card's menu (`role="menu"`, a portal), and from the toaster's viewport: Esc there closes an editor, a correction panel, or a menu, and never ends the session. Space still talks wherever it did, a card included, but never from a control.

**Navigation.** "Inbox" in the header with its count ("Inbox 3", named "Inbox, 3 to review"; no badge at none), and the count on the home page, Today ("3 proposals wait for your review.", with "Open the inbox"), from one count query (`lib/inbox/count.ts`) in the viewer's `withTenant` transaction: the viewer's own debriefs, sealed, their proposals still `proposed` and not snoozed past now, which is what the inbox shows for review (it counts every such debrief, where the inbox's first page reads the latest 20). It costs one tenant transaction per server render that shows it (five statements on one pooled connection: `BEGIN`, the role, the two settings, the count, `COMMIT`), on the `(workspace_id, status)` index, shared by the header and Today through React's `cache`, and it streams in behind the header (`Suspense`), so it never holds up a page; a failed count shows "Inbox" alone. Client navigation doesn't render the layout again, so the inbox publishes its own count as its reviews land (`lib/inbox/count-store.ts`), once it holds every debrief, and the header shows that until the next server render's count.

### Diff cards, 3-way, and re-linking (WP12)

**What the loader adds** (`lib/inbox/load.ts`, `lib/inbox/crm.ts`). For each update, a deal update's stage, amount, and close date and a linked contact's title, email, and phone, the fields whose value in the CRM moved since the proposal was made: `InboxProposal.crm.moved[field]`, the value then (`base`, `valueAt`'s rule over `crm_entity_changes` at the `proposal.created` event's time) and when it last changed. The value now is the record's, as WP5 read it (`InboxEntity.values`). One history query for every field of every debrief loaded; superseded proposals are skipped. Entities say whether they're demo records, and the data whether the workspace's mirror is the demo CRM. The tray and the pilot never set `crm`, so their cards are as they were: `pilot.v1` and the sample debriefs as loaded show no 3-way card and no re-link offer, and the scripted reviewer's KLM baseline doesn't move.

**The 3-way card**, only when a field's value now differs from its `base`. The field's row reads the spec's copy: "Appointment scheduled when you spoke · Qualified to buy in the CRM now (changed 2 h ago) · you said proposal" (`ThreeWayValue`; the dots are hidden from screen readers, which hear commas; "2 h ago" from the page's clock, which reads no time zone, so the server's render and the page's agree). The card is loud at review time: amber, "Needs a check", and, where no flag's loud line leads, the line "The stage changed in your CRM after you spoke. Keep yours or the CRM's?", which its description repeats. Its actions:

- **Keep mine** (the Accept button, A, and 1 when the card has no choices of its own): an approval, as proposed.
- **Keep the CRM's** (2, likewise): an `edited` review whose `editDiff` sets each moved field from the proposed value to the CRM's value now ("Kept the CRM's stage: deal update for Acme Dynamics - Expansion."). A field whose value now is the one proposed changes nothing, so it's left out; with nothing left, keeping the CRM's is keeping the rep's, and the card is approved.
- **Edit**, Reject, and More, as on every card.

**The re-link offer** (`lib/inbox/relink.ts`), for proposals still open whose target is a new record named by words. The loader first asks whether any record arrived (its name's change from null, ADR-0008) since the oldest such proposal: one small query, and on most loads the end of it. Only then does it read the mirror (`loadCrmEntities` with no focus account: the most recent 250 of each kind, the reads `POST /api/sessions` makes) and build one link index per load, and for each proposal run `link()` (`@earshot/understanding/link`) with the context SessionDO's linker had: the session's focus account (when the index holds it), the records linked earlier in the session (`withRecentLinks` over its proposals before this one), the kinds its type allows, and linker v1's parameters, from `@earshot/agent/link-context`, which brings no extractor. A record is offered only when the linker links (not asks or proposes new again), and only when its arrival is in the history after the proposal's time, since the copy says it was "added to your CRM after you spoke". The card is loud, with §4.4's line, "Atmo Systems was added to your CRM after you spoke. Is it this one?", and numbered choices, "Which record?": the record ("added after you spoke"), its sound-alikes at p ≥ 0.05, and "A new record". It needs an answer, as a question does: Accept with nothing picked says "Choose a record first", and c goes to the choices, as WP5's record choice does. Choosing the record approves with `target` set to it; "A new record" approves the new record. Either way `seen.relinked` names the record offered.

**Neither is a flag.** `tierOf`, the evals, their budgets, and every baseline are unchanged; the card's tier as shown (`CardModel.tier`, `seen.tier`) is loud. "Accept all" leaves both out ("Needs a check: The stage changed in your CRM after you spoke."), and the writers agree: `checkReview` refuses a batch approval whose `seen` records a CRM change (a field's `current`) or a re-link offer (`seenLoudAtReview`), and a policy's likewise. No policy could take either anyway: a policy runs once, as `SessionDO` creates the proposal, before anything could have moved, and never on a new record (`not_linked`).

**Trusting a re-link.** `seen.relinked` counts as offered only on a new-record proposal (`checkReview`'s target rule; before WP12 it widened any proposal's choices), and the review route refuses one that isn't a record the workspace's mirror holds, of a kind the proposal's type can target (400), so a page can't reach a record nothing offered, one that isn't there, or one of the wrong kind.

**What each review records** now (`seenOf`): each update field's `base` (the history's value at the proposal's time, else the value now), its `current` only where it moved, any record offered (`relinked`), and the tier as shown.

**The demo controls** (`lib/inbox/demo.ts`, `app/actions/inbox-demo.ts`), in More under "Demo CRM", and only where the card's record is a demo record:

- **"Simulate a CRM change"** on an open update linked to a demo record that holds one of its fields: it moves the first such field one step (`demoMove` in `@earshot/db`): a deal's next stage (a closed one reopened at contract sent, a stage the pipeline doesn't know to qualified to buy), the amount up 10% to a whole number, the close date a week later, a contact's new title (the first of three fixed titles that differs), the email with a 2 after its name, the next phone number in the demo CRM's 555-01xx range. The property and its history row are written in one transaction, the record locked first.
- **"Add a matching record"** on an open new-record proposal for a company or contact, in a workspace whose mirror is the demo CRM (demo records, and no CRM connected), with no offer yet: it adds the record under the words heard (a contact with the company said beside it, when that's a demo company), its arrival recorded in the same transaction, once per kind and name.

Both are server actions that take only the card's ids, find the debrief the viewer's own or do nothing, and act through the data layer's writers, which touch only the workspace's demo rows (no connection, the `demo:` prefix; added records are `demo:added:…`, so removing the demo CRM removes them too). The page hands them to the view as props, so the client view and its stories never import server code; it says the result in one polite message and reads the inbox again, and the card shows its three values or its offer. Each writes an audit entry with ids and the field or kind, never values.

**The keys.** The help dialog's digits say what 1 and 2 do on a 3-way card.

**The web Worker's bundle** (`build:worker`, then `wrangler deploy --dry-run`): 23,471.37 KiB, gzip 5,066.71 KiB, before WP12 (f8241a6); 23,693.87 KiB, gzip 5,126.60 KiB, after (8c2b9cb): +222.50 KiB, +59.89 KiB gzipped, for the linker and the name toolkit (`@earshot/understanding/link`), the linker's context (`@earshot/agent/link-context`), and WP12's own code. That's under the 250 KiB gzipped the brief allowed, and the Worker stays under 10 MiB gzipped, so re-links run in the web Worker, not through the edge Worker by RPC (spec §8). Builds vary by some kilobytes: one midway (8e59df8, with a little less code) measured 23,707.00 KiB, gzip 5,129.61 KiB. The edge Worker's dry-run bundle is 2,852.32 KiB, gzip 546.72 KiB, +2.19 KiB and +0.62 KiB gzipped over ADR-0034's, for the review rules.

### Where WP12 departs from the spec

1. **The 3-way card is loud at review time too.** The spec makes only the re-link offer loud. A CRM change after the rep spoke is a conflict with the CRM, and loud is what keeps a card out of "accept all": approving it unseen would keep the rep's value over the CRM's without a look. It isn't a flag either.
2. **The 3-way card's line is Earshot's.** The spec gives only the field's row; the line follows the re-link offer's form. The row shows the proposed value as stored ("you said proposal").
3. **Keep the CRM's over several fields** edits each moved field; a field whose CRM value is the one proposed is left out, and with none left it's an approval, since an edit must change something (ADR-0032).
4. **Keys:** 1 and 2 on a 3-way card with no choices of its own; a card with choices keeps its digits for them.
5. **A re-link needs the record's arrival after the proposal**, in the mirror's history, as well as a link, so the copy is true; a record with no arrival row counts as always there. The spec says "if it links now".
6. **The re-link's index** is the mirror as `loadCrmEntities` reads it with no focus account (up to 250 records of each kind), not the 250 a session's `start` holds; its turn mentions are empty, since proposals don't record their turn (the company said with a contact still counts); and the earlier links come from the session's proposals as folded, so a witness downgrade isn't one. A record past the 250 most recent of its kind waits for Phase 6's `searchEntities`.
7. **The re-link card needs an answer**, as a question does, rather than Accept creating the new record.
8. **`seen.relinked` widens only a new record's choices**, and the route checks the record is in the mirror.
9. **"Simulate a CRM change" is on update cards only** (deal updates and contacts on demo records), where a moved field can show; the spec says cards whose record is a demo record. **"Add a matching record"** is offered only where the mirror is the demo CRM, and is idempotent by kind and name.
10. **The demo controls aren't development-only:** the demo CRM is how anyone tries Earshot before connecting a CRM, and they act only on its records.

## Consequences

**Good**

- One rule table decides what every surface marks and what "accept all" and policies take; the writers can't disagree with the cards.
- The marks are measured and gated at $0 on every PR: loud, marked, surfacing, with precision and hidden wrong accounts reported.
- Loud marks are precise on the synthetic set (65.5%), and every seeded error carries a marker.
- The badge share falls from 45.8% to 30.1% without changing a decision, since `link_uncertain` moved beside the name.
- Doubt is phrased in the first person only where Earshot has something to settle, and the copy is testable.
- The inbox works by key, pointer, and screen reader alike: every action is a native button, the keys are only a faster way to them, and they act only where focus says they should.
- A stray digit can't relink a record: only a card that asks offers numbered records, and a linked card's others live behind c.
- One region speaks, once per action; failures interrupt, nothing else does.
- Each review carries what the pilot and Phase 6 need (`seen`, `timing`, `input`, `surface`), from one clock with fake-clock tests.
- The page's own reviews show at once and survive refusals: the card comes back with a message, and nothing queued behind it is sent.
- "Accept all" can't take a card the writers would refuse: its set is `checkReview`'s, tested card by card on a fixture with every tier, and the checkpoint says what stays and why before anything is written.
- The inbox and the tray are one engine: the same keys, focus rules, messages, undo, and timings, so the tray's reviews carry what the pilot and Phase 6 need too, and a fix lands in both.
- A card shows the CRM's value when the rep spoke beside its value now (WP12), so a change made since is a person's decision, Keep mine or Keep the CRM's, by key, never an overwrite, and each review records what it saw (`base`, `current`) for Phase 6's conflict check.
- A new record that has reached the CRM since the rep spoke is offered before it becomes a duplicate, and only when the mirror's history says it really arrived after.
- The demo controls let anyone see both on the demo CRM, and touch nothing else.

**Bad**

- **The loud budget isn't met:** it's gated at an interim 18%, against the spec's 15%, until false witness conflicts fall, which needs the mined set and a linker or witness change.
- Synthetic precision flatters: the cassettes are the ground truth plus seeded errors, so quiet notes are 0% precise by construction, and loud precision says nothing yet about real speech (spec Appendix B, #12).
- A wrong account linked by context or sound above p 0.97 carries no marker at all (d037). Making sound links loud, or a sensitivity setting, are candidates after the pilot (spec §8), not before.
- Linker asks are imprecise here (37.5%): linker v1 asks where the scripts' context rule links. They stay loud, since an ask can't be accepted as it stands.
- An unsupported target's line can't quote the model's words, which the proposal doesn't keep.
- The name note makes 118 proposals (15.7%) ineligible for "accept all" though only one of them was wrong: recall for wrong accounts, bought with review time.
- Keys need focus in the list: arriving on the page, a keyboard user tabs to the first card (or clicks one) before j and a do anything. That's SC 2.1.4's price for keys that are on by default.
- NVDA's browse mode keeps several of the letters and the digits; its users press NVDA+Space or Tab to the buttons. Whether NVDA and JAWS stay in focus mode as j and k move between non-widget cards is WP16's to find out (spec Appendix B, #3).
- Base UI 1.9 announces an urgent toast through its own visually hidden `role="alert"` copy and hides the visible toast with `aria-hidden` until focus enters the region, though it stays focusable; axe flags that (`aria-hidden-focus`). No state the gate lists shows a failure, and the one story that does turns that rule off, with the reason. Upstream, or a toast that leaves the tab order while hidden, would fix it.
- `seen.fields[].base` reads the mirror's history (WP12), which only the demo CRM's controls write until Phase 6's sync does: on a real CRM before then, a value that moved without a history row shows no 3-way card, and its `base` is its value now.
- A 3-way card or a re-link offer costs a review that "accept all" would have taken: by design, since each is a decision about the CRM, not a mark the evals count.
- Re-links read the mirror and build a link index in the web Worker on any load where a record arrived since the oldest open new-record proposal (+59.89 KiB gzipped to its bundle), and only over the 250 most recent records of each kind.
- The re-link offer depends on arrivals being recorded: a record that reached the mirror without one (the demo CRM's dataset, or a sync that skips it) is never offered.
- An undo or a reopen records zero timing.
- Optimism means a review can be lost if the tab closes in the instant it's sent; the page asks first while anything is on its way.
- A batch longer than one request (25 cards, or 64 KiB) isn't atomic: refused partway, the part that landed stays accepted, and the message says how many. Real debriefs make a handful of proposals, so one request holds a batch in practice.
- The batch takes the name note's cards out too (quiet), so on the 200 debriefs about half of all proposals can be accepted together (54.2% are unmarked), and a debrief whose cards are all marked gets a disabled button.
- The header's count is the server's at each full render, and the inbox's own while it's open; elsewhere it can lag a session that sealed since (the tray's reviews only count once sealed). The count reads every sealed debrief, where the inbox's first page reads 20.
- After the seal the tray's reviews go to the route, which takes them once Postgres holds the seal: right after it, a review can come back "still finishing", and zero-setup development, which has no flush, never gets that far.
